A music visualizer turns sound into moving pictures: bars that rise with the beat, a wave that traces the sound, stars that glow as it plays. Most come with a music player and follow one song. With a soundscape of your own sounds, like rain, a fan or a stream, a visualizer can follow each sound: where it is, how loud it is, and what colour you gave it. Some follow every sound closely; others take your colours and move more gently, and are simply nice to look at while you listen. Here's how visualizers differ from animated scenes, how to watch one at night, and how to try ours.

## What a music visualizer is

A visualizer listens to what's playing and draws it, many times a second. The simplest show the sound's level, like a meter. Others turn the sound into shapes, colours and motion: rings on water, curtains of light, a flight through stars. How closely they follow varies a lot: a meter shows every change, while a slow aurora mostly takes the colours and drifts. Both have their place: one to watch your sounds, the other to have something easy to look at.

## Watching at night: set up your screen

A bright screen in a dark room is a lot. A few settings help:

1. **Turn the brightness down.** On iPhone, open Control Center and drag the brightness control down. On a Pixel phone, open Settings > Display & touch, tap Brightness level and move the slider; other Android phones have it under Display.
2. **Warm the colours.** On iPhone, turn on Night Shift in Settings > Display & Brightness. On a Pixel phone, turn on Night Light in Settings > Display & touch; other Android phones call it something similar, under Display.
3. **Let the picture end.** A visualizer usually keeps the screen on while it draws. Look for one that can fade to black after a while, so the screen doesn't stay on all night while your sounds play.
4. **Choose slow and dark.** If bright or fast-moving light bothers you, pick slow pictures with dark backgrounds, and stop if anything feels uncomfortable.

## How to choose

- **Want to see the sound itself?** Pick a classic one: bars or a trace that rises and falls with it.
- **Watching in the evening?** Pick something dark and slow, like stars, an aurora or a fire, and set a fade to black.
- **Want somewhere to be, not a meter?** Pick an animated scene, and let your own sounds play over it.
- **Watching for a while?** Turn your phone sideways for a bigger picture, and keep it on a charger.

## Questions people ask

### Can I use a music visualizer with my own sounds?
Yes, if the app plays your sounds as well as drawing them. Many visualizers are made for music players and follow whatever song is on. In the Effugium Aura beta, Visualize draws the sounds in your own mix, rain, a fan, a stream, or anything you've recorded or imported: some visualizers show each sound plainly, others just take its colours and move gently.

### What's the difference between a visualizer and an animated scene?
A visualizer draws the sound itself and changes as it changes. An animated scene is a place to watch, like a fire at night, that plays on its own while your sounds play over it.

### Will a visualizer run down my battery?
It keeps the screen on and draws all the time, so it uses more battery than sounds alone. Turn the brightness down, keep your phone on a charger for long sessions, and use a fade to black.

### Can I watch a visualizer in bed?
Yes. Turn the brightness down, warm the colours with Night Shift or Night Light, pick something dark and slow, and set it to fade to black.

### Do visualizers flash?
Some do. Read the app's note about light sensitivity, choose slower pictures and gentler motion if it has them, and stop if anything feels uncomfortable.

## Bottom line

A visualizer shows your sounds as moving pictures; a scene gives you somewhere to look while they play. For the evening, pick something dark and slow, dim the screen, and let it fade to black while your sounds carry on.
