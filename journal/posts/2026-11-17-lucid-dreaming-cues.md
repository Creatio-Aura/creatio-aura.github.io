A lucid dreaming cue is a signal you practise with: a soft sound, a tap on your wrist, or both, played on a timer while you sleep. A lucid dream is one where you realize you're dreaming, and some people who practise for it pair a cue with an awareness check before bed, then let the same cue play quietly later in the night. Here's how that practice works, how to set up a cue on a timer, and why I use the same cue when I meditate.

## Why I use a cue: meditating, sleeping and lucid dreaming

I'm Andrew, and I make Effugium Aura. For me, meditation, sleep and lucid dreaming sit very close together, and the line between them is easy to cross without meaning to.

When I meditate, I don't want to fall asleep, but sometimes it happens. Having a cue, a sound I can hear or a tap I can feel, every so often gives me a nudge to wake up and come back to the meditation.

Other nights I'm trying to lucid dream and slip into ordinary sleep instead. There, a cue that I can hear, feel or both stirs me just lightly, so I can carry on trying.

That's why we built the Kicker into Effugium Aura: one cue on a timer that you set, as a sound, a vibration or both. It's named after the "kick" in the film Inception.

## What a cue is

The practice is simple. Before you sleep, you rehearse a habit: pausing to notice your surroundings and asking yourself, "Am I dreaming?" Each time, the same sound plays. Later, the sound plays again, softly, while you sleep. The sound is your reminder to ask the question.

There's no promise in any of it. Lucid dreaming is something people practise, and it comes to some people more than others.

## How to practise with a cue

1. **Pick one soft sound and keep it for this**: a chime, a bell or a few soft notes. A sound you hear all day won't stand out.
2. **Practise before bed.** For a few minutes, play the sound now and then. Each time, stop, look around, notice your body, and ask yourself whether you're dreaming.
3. **Let it play quietly later in the night.** Many people who practise this use the last hours of the night, or the time after waking briefly and going back to bed.
4. **Keep it soft.** The cue should be gentle, not an alarm.
5. **Keep a dream journal.** Write down what you remember as soon as you wake, so you can see what changes over time.

Your rest comes first. If the cues are costing you sleep, play them less often, or stop.

## Sound, vibration or both?

It's your choice, and you can change it any night.

| Cue | What it's like | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A sound | Easy to pair with your practice before bed | Sleeping alone, or meditating with headphones |
| A vibration | Silent: only you feel it, on your wrist or from the phone | Sharing a room or a bed |
| Both | The sound and the tap together, or a tap first as a heads-up | A cue you'd like to notice whichever way |

## Before you sleep: set up your phone and watch

1. **Check Focus and Do Not Disturb.** On Apple Watch, Do Not Disturb stops both the sound and the tap of notifications, so a cue that reaches your watch as a notification can be held back until morning. If you use Sleep Focus on iPhone, check whether the app playing your cue is allowed through it.
2. **On Apple Watch, use Silent Mode if you want taps but no chimes.** Press the side button to open Control Center, then tap the Silent Mode button, the bell. Apple says you still get the taps in Silent Mode.
3. **Set the volume low**, then raise it a little if you sleep through every cue.
4. **Try it once with the screen locked** before a whole night, so you know the cue gets through.

## How to choose

- **Practising for lucid dreams?** Use the same soft sound before bed and at night. Start with a long gap between cues, and shorten it if you'd like more.
- **Meditating, and you sometimes drift off?** Set a cue every few minutes, the way you'd set a [meditation interval bell](/journal/meditation-interval-bell/).
- **Sharing a room?** Choose a vibration on your wrist.
- **Want both?** A tap a few seconds before the sound gives you a heads-up.

## Questions people ask

### Can a sound make me have a lucid dream?
We can't promise that, and no method works for everyone. A cue is something you practise with, nothing more.

### When in the night should a lucid dreaming cue play?
That's up to you. Many people who practise use the last hours of the night.

### Will the cue wake me up?
It might, if it's loud. Keep it soft. If it keeps waking you, turn it down or play it less often.

### Can I use a cue so I don't fall asleep while meditating?
Yes. Set a soft sound or a tap every few minutes. If you've drifted off, it's your nudge to come back to the meditation.

### Can my Apple Watch give me lucid dreaming cues?
Yes, with an app that taps it on a timer. Check that Do Not Disturb or Sleep Focus doesn't hold the taps back. In the Effugium Aura beta, the Kicker can vibrate your Apple Watch while your iPhone is locked: try it and tell us whether every tap arrived.

## Bottom line

Pick one soft sound, or a tap on your wrist, practise with it before bed, and let it play quietly on a timer later in the night. The same cue works as a nudge if you drift off while meditating. Keep it gentle, and try it once with your phone locked before a whole night.
