If your partner snores, white noise is one of the simplest things to try: a steady sound of your own, so the snoring stands out less. Which noise you pick, where it plays from and how loud all make a difference. Here's what to try, how to set up your phone for the night, and how to try it in our beta.

## Which noise for snoring?

Noise covers a sound best when it shares its pitch, and snoring is mostly low. So start low, and work up:

| Noise | Sounds like | Try it when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Brown** | A deep rumble, like distant surf | The snoring is a low, rolling rumble |
| **Pink** | Steady rain | You'd like something softer than white, but less bassy than brown |
| **White** | A bright hiss, like a fan on high | The snoring still stands out over brown or pink |

Listen to each for a minute at about the same volume, and keep the one you like. A recording of your own fan or air conditioner works too, if you're used to one. For more on how the three differ, see [White vs pink vs brown noise](/journal/white-vs-pink-vs-brown-noise/).

## A speaker, or sleep earbuds?

| | A speaker | Sleep earbuds or headphones |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who hears it | You both | Only you |
| Comfort | Nothing in your ears | Something in your ears all night; side sleepers need a low, soft pair |
| Where to put it | On your side of the bed, pointing toward you | In your ears |
| Good for | A partner who likes the noise too | A partner who'd rather have quiet |

With a speaker, keep it closer to you than to the snoring, so the noise is what's nearest your ears. With earbuds, check they're comfortable lying on your side before a whole night.

## How loud?

Use the lowest volume that covers the snoring, not the loudest you can stand. Start low, turn it up a step at a time until the snoring fades into the background, and stop there. With a speaker, keep it a little way from your head rather than right beside your pillow.

## Set up your phone for the night

1. **Charge it.** A whole night of sound uses battery, so leave it on the charger.
2. **Keep it playing with the screen locked.** Most sleep sound apps do; check yours by locking the phone for a minute before bed.
3. **Decide how it ends:** all night, or a [timer that fades it out](/journal/sleep-sounds-that-fade-out/). If the snoring goes on all night, all night may suit you better.
4. **Turn on Do Not Disturb,** so a message doesn't sound or light up the screen in the night.
5. **No app?** On iPhone, Background Sounds can play steady sounds on their own: Settings > Accessibility > Audio & Visual > Background Sounds.

## How to choose

- **Partner doesn't mind the noise?** A speaker on your side, brown or pink, all night.
- **Partner wants quiet?** Sleep earbuds, and the same noise only you can hear.
- **Snoring comes and goes?** Keep the noise steady all night, so it doesn't stop just as the snoring starts again.
- **Can't settle on one?** Mix them: a low brown noise with rain or your own fan on top.

## Questions people ask

### What noise is best for snoring?
There's no single best one. Snoring is a low sound, so many people start with brown or pink noise and move to white if the snoring still stands out. Listen to each at about the same volume and keep the one you like.

### Is white noise or brown noise better for snoring?
Try brown first: it sits low, like most snoring. White is brighter and can feel harsher, but some people prefer it. It comes down to what you like hearing.

### Should I use earbuds or a speaker?
A speaker if your partner doesn't mind the noise; earbuds or soft sleep headphones if they'd rather have quiet. With a speaker, keep it on your side, nearer you than the snoring.

### How loud should white noise be for snoring?
The lowest level that covers it. Start low and turn it up a step at a time until the snoring fades into the background.

### Should white noise play all night?
It's your choice. If the snoring goes on through the night, all night may suit you. If you only need it while you drift off, use a timer that fades it out.

### Can I play white noise on my iPhone without an app?
Yes. Go to Settings > Accessibility > Audio & Visual > Background Sounds, turn on Background Sounds, then choose a sound. You can also start it from Control Center.

## Bottom line

Start with brown or pink noise, play it on your side of the bed or through sleep earbuds, at the lowest volume that covers the snoring, and keep it going all night if the snoring does. Try a few nights before you settle on one.
