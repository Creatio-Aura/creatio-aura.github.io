You sit down to follow your breath, and a few minutes later you're planning dinner. Everyone who meditates knows it. An interval bell is a cue on a timer: a soft sound, or a tap on your wrist, every few minutes, as a reminder to check where your attention is and come back.

## Wander, notice, come back

When you meditate on the breath, wandering off isn't failing. Many teachers describe it as a loop: your mind wanders, you notice, you come back to the breath, you stay a while, and then it wanders again. Coming back is the practice.

An interval bell is a reminder from outside to check in. Wherever your attention is when it rings, that's your cue to notice it and return.

## How to use one

Many meditation traditions use a bell: a single sound, and a pause to come back to the breath. An interval bell puts that on a timer during your sit.

1. **Set the length of your sit and the interval before you start**, so you don't pick up the phone once you've begun.
2. **When it rings, notice where your attention is.** Planning, remembering, a sound in the room: just name it.
3. **Then come back** to the breath, the body, or whatever you were resting on.
4. **Don't judge it.** Each return is the exercise, the way each lift is the exercise at a gym.

## How often should it ring?

There's no right interval. It's yours to set, so start with a guess and adjust.

| If you… | Try a bell every |
| --- | --- |
| sit for 10 to 20 minutes | 3 to 5 minutes |
| sit for half an hour or more | 10 to 15 minutes |
| find the bell itself distracting | Longer, or a single bell halfway |

Choose a sound you like hearing. Many people pick a singing bowl, a soft bell or a chime that rings out and fades, rather than a sharp beep. Keep it low: loud enough to notice, not so loud that it makes you jump.

## A silent bell: a tap on your wrist

A sound isn't always welcome. You might share a room, sit on a train, or simply prefer silence. A vibration is a silent cue: a tap on your Apple Watch, or a buzz from your phone on your lap. Some apps let you choose one tap or a few in a row, so a bell is easy to tell from a message.

## Before you sit: set up your phone and watch

1. **Turn on Do Not Disturb**, so messages don't buzz in the middle of a sit. On iPhone, open Control Center, touch and hold the button that shows Focus or Do Not Disturb, then tap Do Not Disturb. On Android, swipe down from the top of the screen and tap Do Not Disturb (on some phones it's under Modes).
2. **On Apple Watch, use Silent Mode if you want taps but no chimes.** Press the side button to open Control Center, then tap the Silent Mode button, the bell. Apple says you still get haptic notifications, the taps, in Silent Mode.
3. **Check the bell gets through.** On Apple Watch, Do Not Disturb stops both the sound and the tap of notifications, so a bell that reaches your watch as a notification can be held back. On Android, Do Not Disturb's settings can block or allow alarms and media. Try a two-minute sit with the screen locked before a long one.
4. **Set the volume low**, then raise it if you miss a bell.

## Ways to ring a bell every few minutes

| Way | What you do | What to watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your phone's timer | Set a timer for the length of your sit and pick its sound | It rings once, when it runs out, not every few minutes |
| A meditation timer app | Set the length of the sit and how often the bell rings | Check it rings with the screen locked, and whether it can tap your watch |
| A watch app that taps on a timer | Set the interval, and feel each tap on your wrist | Silent, but check Do Not Disturb doesn't hold the taps back |
| An app that rings a bell over other sounds | Play rain or a stream low, with the bell above it | More to set up, so try it before a long sit |

On Android, the Clock app's timer sound and vibration are under More > Settings, then Timers: tap Timer sound to pick what plays, or Timer vibrate to feel it. Not every phone has the vibrate option.

## How to choose

- **Just want to know when time's up?** Your phone's timer is enough: one bell at the end.
- **Want a bell every few minutes?** Use an app with an interval bell. Every 3 to 5 minutes for short sits, 10 to 15 for long ones.
- **Sharing a room, or out in public?** Choose a tap on your wrist, or a vibration from the phone on your lap.
- **Like a sound in the background?** Look for an app that plays rain or a stream low, with the bell above it.
- **Wearing an Apple Watch?** Silent Mode keeps the taps and mutes the chimes.

## Questions people ask

### Is it OK for my mind to wander during meditation?
Yes. Noticing that it wandered and coming back is the practice itself. A bell is simply a reminder to check.

### How often should a meditation interval bell ring?
There's no right answer. Many people start with every 3 to 5 minutes for short sits and every 10 to 15 minutes for long ones, then adjust.

### Can I set a mindfulness bell every 10 minutes on my phone?
Yes, with a meditation timer app that has an interval bell: set the interval to 10 minutes. Your phone's own timer rings once, when it runs out, so it suits a bell at the end of the sit. In the Effugium Aura beta, you can set the Kicker to every 10 minutes and tell us how it went.

### What sound should I use for an interval bell?
One you like hearing. Many people choose a singing bowl or a soft bell that rings out and fades.

### Can my Apple Watch tap me every few minutes while I meditate?
Yes, with an app that taps it on a timer. Put the watch in Silent Mode to keep the taps and mute the chimes; Do Not Disturb stops both, so check that the taps get through. In the Effugium Aura beta, the Kicker can vibrate your Apple Watch while your iPhone is locked. Try it and tell us whether every tap arrived.

### Will the bell ring with my phone locked?
That depends on the app, so try a short sit with the screen locked before a long one. In the Effugium Aura beta, it's one of the things we're testing: tell us if a bell didn't come.

### Can I meditate with rain or other sounds playing?
Yes, if you like. Keep them low, and let the bell ring above them.

## Bottom line

Pick a sound you like, or a tap on your wrist, and set it to every few minutes. Each time it comes, notice where your attention is and come back. Try a short sit first, to check the bell gets through Do Not Disturb and a locked screen.
