Effugium Aura · Test version

Thanks for testing Effugium Aura

Effugium Aura plays sounds around you: your own recordings and built-in ones, each in its own place, for sleep, focus or quiet moments. You choose the sounds, where they sit, when a cue comes, and how it ends. This is a test version, so things will change, and your notes shape what it becomes.

The short version

Install it from the link Andrew sent you, put on headphones, drag a few sounds onto the circle, and press Play. Then try one thing from the list below each night, and tell us how it went.

Installing it

  • iPhone: the link opens TestFlight, Apple's app for test versions. Install TestFlight if asked, then tap Install for Effugium Aura. Updates arrive in TestFlight.
  • Android: first send Andrew the Google account email you use on your phone (usually a Gmail address), so he can add you. Then open the Google Play link he sends on your phone, tap Accept invite, then Download it on Google Play. Updates arrive through Google Play.

What's different in a test version

  • Everything is unlocked, including the parts that will be paid later. Nothing you do in the test version charges you.
  • It will change. Some screens are early, and some wording is placeholder.
  • On iPhone, a test version lasts 90 days. A new one usually arrives before then.

Try it · Your own sounds

Your own fan, anywhere

If you're used to a particular fan, air conditioner or the rain on your window, record it once and take it with you.

Record it

  1. Set your phone down near the sound, not touching it, and turn on Do Not Disturb.
  2. In Effugium, tap Record (beside Import), or ☰ > Field recorder.
  3. Record for a minute or two. The tape marks the point where there's enough to loop.
  4. Trim it, roll it up, and choose Make seamless to smooth the join.

Already have a recording? Tap Import to bring it in from your phone's files.

Place it

  1. Drag your new sound onto a place around the circle, where the fan sits in your room.
  2. Add anything else you like: rain behind you, a low noise underneath.
  3. Put on headphones and press Play.

Want to fine-tune the join? ☰ > Loop Studio lets you choose it yourself and hear Before and After.

Try it · Spatial sound

Sounds around you

Each sound can sit in its own place: in front, behind, to the side, above or below. On headphones, the mix sounds like a room around you rather than a track inside your head.

Place your sounds

  1. Put on headphones. On speakers, sounds play in plain stereo.
  2. Drag sounds from the list onto the places around the circle.
  3. Drag a sound to move it, and tap it for its volume and more.

Set some moving

  1. Switch from Flat to Gravity at the top.
  2. Tap a sound and choose Orbit to send it slowly around you. Random gives each its own way; Reset puts everything back.
  3. Under Shooting stars, tap Add for a sound that passes by now and then.

On iPhone with AirPods

On iPhone, Effugium uses Apple's spatial audio on headphones, with your Personalized Spatial Audio profile if you've made one. With AirPods that support it, head tracking keeps sounds in place as you turn your head. Turn it off in ☰ > Head tracking if you prefer sounds that turn with you.

Try it · Meditation

A bell to come back to

The Kicker is a cue on a timer, above everything else. While you meditate, it can be a soft bell every few minutes, or a silent vibration on your phone or wrist: a reminder to notice where your attention is and come back.

Set it up

  1. Make a quiet soundscape, or pick one, and switch to Gravity. The Kicker plays in Gravity while a mix plays.
  2. Under Kicker, tap Add.
  3. Kick with: Sound, Vibrate or Both.
  4. Vibrate on: Phone, Watch or Both (Watch shows if your iPhone has an Apple Watch paired). On Android, it vibrates your phone.
  5. How often: try every 3 to 5 minutes for a short sit, longer for a long one.
  6. Choose a sound, like the singing bowl, and tap Try it now.

On your Apple Watch

The watch vibrates while your iPhone is locked, through quiet notifications named Kick 1, Kick 2, Kick 3. Allow notifications when asked. If your watch chimes as well, turn on its Silent Mode: press the side button, then tap the bell. The Kicker's own checklist has a test you can send.

Try it · Dreams

Cues for dream practice

Some people who practise lucid dreaming use a cue: one soft sound they pair with an awareness check before bed, then hear again quietly later in the night. The Kicker can be that cue. It doesn't make anything happen; it's a cue you control.

Set it up

  1. Pick one soft sound for this, like wind chimes, and keep it for this.
  2. Before bed, play it a few times with Try it now. Each time, look around and ask yourself whether you're dreaming.
  3. For the night, add the Kicker in Gravity: soft, every 20 minutes or so, or vibrate only on your watch so nobody else hears it.
  4. Keep the volume low, and keep a note of what you remember when you wake.

Your rest comes first. If the cue wakes you, turn it down, space it out, or switch it off. Effugium plays on a timer you set; it can't tell when you're dreaming.

Try it · Sleep timer

How the night ends

Play your mix all night, or set a sleep timer that fades it slowly to silence. It's your call.

The sleep timer

  1. ☰ > Sleep timer, and pick a length.
  2. At the end, the mix fades slowly to silence, then stops. The fade lengthens with the timer, up to ten minutes.
  3. It keeps working with the screen off.

From the lock screen (iPhone)

While a mix plays, a panel shows on your lock screen and in StandBy, with Pause, Quieter, Louder and Fade out now. Fade out now fades the mix over a minute; tap Cancel fade out to change your mind. The buttons should work without unlocking your phone.

Playback controls (Android)

The first time you press Play, Android asks whether Effugium Aura can send notifications. Choose Allow: that's how its playback controls show on your lock screen and when you swipe down from the top of the screen.

Volume

The volume slider in the app is your phone's own volume: the side buttons move it, and so do Quieter and Louder on the lock screen and the crown on your Apple Watch.

Try it · Sleep sounds

White, pink and brown noise

All three are a steady hiss made of every pitch at once. White sounds like a hiss, pink like steady rain, brown like a low rumble. There's no right one: listen and pick yours.

Hear the difference

  1. Place White, Pink and Brown (in the Noise folder) one at a time, at the same volume.
  2. Listen to each for a minute, and keep the one you like.
  3. Try mixing: a low brown noise underneath, rain on top, each in its own place.
NoiseFrom low to high pitchesSounds like
WhiteThe same strength at every pitchA hiss, like a fan on high
PinkSofter as the pitch risesSteady rain
BrownSofter still in the highsA low rumble, like distant surf

For a baby's room, keep the phone or speaker well away from the crib and the volume low, and ask your pediatrician.
