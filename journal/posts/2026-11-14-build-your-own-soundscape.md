A soundscape is a few sounds played together: rain with a fire under it, a fan with a stream in the distance, the sea with birds overhead. Most sleep sound apps come with ready-made mixes. A custom soundscape app, or a custom sleep sound mixer, lets you build your own: pick the sounds, set each one's volume, and keep the mix for another night. Here's how to build a soundscape of your own, and what to look for in a customized audio player for it.

## What goes into a soundscape

| Layer | What it does in the mix | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A base** | One steady sound that plays the whole time | Brown or pink noise, a fan, steady rain, an air conditioner |
| **A middle layer** | A sound with a little movement, quieter than the base | A fire, a stream, wind, waves |
| **Details** | Something small that comes and goes, quietest of all | Birds, distant thunder, a clock, a singing bowl now and then |

Two or three layers is plenty. More than that and they start to blur into each other.

## Build your own soundscape, step by step

1. **Start with the base.** Pick one steady sound and set it at a comfortable, low volume. Everything else sits on top of it.
2. **Add one layer at a time.** Bring in a second sound and turn it down until you only just notice it. Then try a third, the same way.
3. **Use your own recordings.** The sound you know best, your own fan, the rain on your roof, a creek you walk past, can be any of the layers. [Your own fan, anywhere](/journal/record-your-fan-noise/) shows how to record one.
4. **Check the loops.** A recording that repeats with a click or a gap will stand out in the mix. Pick a player that helps it loop more seamlessly.
5. **Give each sound its own place.** On headphones, a sound on your left and another behind you are easier to tell apart than two sounds in the middle of your head.
6. **Listen for a few minutes**, then change one thing at a time: a volume, a sound, a place.
7. **Keep it.** Save the mix, so it's there for next time without building it again.

## How to choose a custom sound mixer app

- **Want your own sounds in it?** Check the app imports recordings or records them itself, and loops them smoothly.
- **Want the layers to stay apart?** Look for one that places sounds around you on headphones, not only side by side.
- **Using it at night?** Make sure it keeps playing with the screen off, and has a timer that fades out.
- **Building more than one?** Check how many mixes it keeps, and whether you can name them.
- **Want to share one?** Some apps let you send a mix to someone else.

## Questions people ask

### What is a soundscape?
A few sounds played together as one mix, such as rain with a fire under it, or a fan with a stream in the distance. Some are recorded in one place; others are built by layering separate sounds.

### How do I make my own soundscape?
Start with one steady sound, add one or two quieter layers on top, and set each one's volume so the mix stays even. Use your own recordings for any layer, and save the mix so you can play it again.

### Is there a sleep sound app where I can mix my own sounds?
Yes, several sleep sound apps let you mix sounds, and some let you import or record your own. In the Effugium Aura beta you can mix your own recordings with built-in sounds, and place each one around you.

### How many sounds should a soundscape have?
Two or three is plenty for most people: a steady base, a quieter layer, and maybe one small detail. More than that tends to blur together.

### Can I share a soundscape I made?
Some apps let you. In the newest Effugium Aura test version, you can send a whole soundscape, with the sounds of your own it uses, through your phone's share sheet, and it opens in Effugium for the person you send it to.

## Bottom line

To build your own soundscape, start with a steady base, add a layer or two at a time, keep the volumes even, and give each sound its own place. With your own recordings in the mix, it's a soundscape no one else has.
